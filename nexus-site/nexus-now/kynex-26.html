01 THE EVENT

KYNEX '26 was a robotics competition built and run by NEXUS Robotics — two distinct battles staged in one arena on a single day.

Teams of undergraduate builders brought one machine each: a 5 kg Sumo gladiator for the Dohyo, or a fully autonomous Line Follower for the confidential track. Every bot passed a technical inspection — size, weight, weapons and safety — before it was allowed to compete.

The event was coordinated by Paarth Mehta and Arpita Bhalekar, with queries, appeals and rulings handled by event officials on the day.

02 THE CHALLENGE

ROBO SUMO

A test of push, poise and power. Two bots entered the ring — only one held ground. Matches ran across four timed rounds (1:15 / 1:00 / 1:00 / 0:45) with a 0:30 tiebreaker round, plus one optional 3-minute technical timeout per match.

A ring-out — pushing the opponent completely off the Dohyo — took the round outright with 200 points. Otherwise, zone position at the whistle decided it: centre 100, middle 50, outer 25.

LINE FOLLOWING

One track, one line, zero margin for error. Fully autonomous racers read a 2 cm black line across a white matte surface — through sharp turns up to 90°, zigzags, curves, dashed segments and intersections.

Each team got one official run per round inside a 3-minute limit with a 45-second calibration window. Ranking was strict: completion first, then time — with 5 seconds added for every human touch.

03 THE ARENA

The Sumo Dohyo: a wooden ring with three concentric scoring zones — centre, middle and outer — fitting, boundary included, within a 7 ft × 7 ft square footprint. The Line Following arena measured 2.5 m × 2.5 m, its layout kept strictly confidential until reveal.

Event-day photography and match footage from both arenas will be archived here as it is published.
